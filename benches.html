Sixteen benches · One adjudicating authority

Where insolvency law actually meets the system.

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code is adjudicated by sixteen benches of the National Company Law Tribunal, spread across India. Each has its own jurisdiction, its own member composition, and — though IBBI does not publish bench-level disposal data — its own reputation for backlog, sectoral specialisation, and procedural culture. This page maps what is publicly known about each bench, and is honest about what is not.

§01 — Bench landscape

The institution at a glance.

Cases are filed before the bench that has jurisdiction over the state where the company's registered office sits. The tribunal has grown from eleven benches in 2016 to sixteen today, with members appointed in tranches and current strength running below sanction.

§03 — Sectoral picture

What the cases are actually about.

The IBBI does not publish a bench-by-sector cross-tabulation. What it does publish is an all-India sectoral breakdown of ongoing CIRPs and liquidations, updated through Parliament replies and quarterly newsletters. Two sectors dominate: manufacturing and real estate.

Of the 1,926 ongoing CIRPs reported by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs as of 31 March 2025, manufacturing accounts for 28% and real estate (including renting & business activities) for 26%. Together, two sectors carry over half the active pipeline. The liquidation pipeline is similar in shape: manufacturing 38%, real estate 18%.

The IBBI footnote
Sectoral data is published all-India only. A real-estate CIRP at NCLT Mumbai is not separately counted from one at NCLT Allahabad in any public source. Bench × sector cross-tabs would have to be reconstructed case-by-case from order PDFs.

§04 — The data we cannot show

What no public source currently publishes.

A bench-by-bench performance table — admissions per quarter, average days from filing to admission, average days from admission to disposal, sectoral share by bench — would be the single most useful artefact for the IBC research community. None of it is published anywhere in the public domain. Where it has surfaced, it has been through Parliament replies, RTI responses, or empirical research extracting data from order PDFs.

Not in IBBI newsletters
  • Bench-wise CIRP admissions per quarter
  • Bench-wise CIRP disposal per quarter
  • Bench-wise plan-approval delay
  • Bench-wise sectoral composition
  • Bench-wise CoC-vote-to-NCLT-order gap
Where it does appear
  • Lok Sabha questions (aggregate pendency, occasional bench breakdown)
  • Standing Committee on Finance reports (32nd Report 2020–21)
  • DAKSH State of Tribunals series
  • Vidhi Centre empirical studies (order-PDF scraping)
  • IIM-A and academic studies (one-off)
Why this matters
The Supreme Court's suo motu cognisance on 29 April 2026 cited 383 pending plan-approval matters and the "grim and dismal" state of NCLT capacity, but the order did not break those 383 down by bench. Until that disaggregation is published — either by IBBI in its newsletters or by MCA in answer to Parliament — public-interest analysis of bench-level performance has to be reconstructed from primary records.
